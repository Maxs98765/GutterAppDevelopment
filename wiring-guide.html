About the * pins: kUnoTxPin (37) and kUnoRxPin (38) in config.h are placeholders, not confirmed values for your specific P4 board — different P4 boards route their GPIO differently, and the C6 occupies a fixed SDIO block you must not collide with. Check your board's schematic before wiring, update these two constants in config.h to match, and only then wire D10/D11 accordingly. The motor-side pins (D9/D6/D7) are ordinary Uno digital pins and free to pick — just avoid D10/D11 (the P4 link) and stay clear of any pins your specific shield or add-on already claims.

Six things worth reading before you pick up a wire

Why only one serial direction needs a divider

The Uno runs its logic at 5V; the P4 only tolerates 3.3V. A wire straight from the Uno's TX pin can put 5V on a P4 input and damage it. The other direction is safe as-is: the P4's 3.3V output already reads as a clear logic-high on the Uno's 5V side.

This motor has no built-in position control

A hobby servo has electronics inside that turn a PWM pulse into a shaft angle. This is a bare 2-wire DC gear motor — it only knows "on," "off," and "which way." The sketch gets to 120° by running it for a calculated time at its rated 16 RPM, not by telling it an angle directly.

Timed positioning drifts — recalibrate if it's off

Real motor speed varies with load and supply voltage, so the 16 RPM label is a starting estimate, not a guarantee. If the arm visibly over- or under-shoots, measure the actual travel with a protractor and adjust MOTOR_RPM in the sketch rather than the wiring.

The ENA jumper has to come off

Most L298N boards ship with a jumper that ties ENA permanently high. Leave it in and the Uno's D9 pin is simply ignored — the motor will run at full tilt the instant IN1/IN2 are set, and the timed sweep won't work at all. Pull the jumper before wiring D9 in.

Size the 12V supply for stall current, not running current

A gear motor draws far more current the moment it's asked to move (or if it stalls against something) than while spinning freely. Undersizing the supply is the most common cause of a driver board or Uno resetting the instant the motor is commanded.

Only one USB controller is "host" at a time

Unrelated to the motor, but still true: the P4 has two USB 2.0 OTG peripherals — one Hi-Speed, one Full-Speed — and only one can act as host in the current software. An older webcam is almost always Full-Speed; pick the matching controller under idf.py menuconfig.
